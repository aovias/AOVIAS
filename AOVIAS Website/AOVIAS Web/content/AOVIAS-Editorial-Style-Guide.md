# AOVIAS Editorial Style Guide

**Status:** Canonical reference for Public Website, documentation, and product marketing copy  
**Audience:** Writers, editors, product marketers, engineers shipping user-facing strings  
**Principle:** Purpose before promotion. Technology exists to strengthen institutions.

---

## 1. Brand voice

AOVIAS speaks with **clarity, precision, confidence, and responsibility**.

| Attribute | Means | Does not mean |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clear | Short sentences; one idea per sentence when possible | Oversimplifying hard topics |
| Precise | Specific nouns; named products and roles | Vague “solutions” and “synergies” |
| Confident | Steady assertions grounded in how we build | Hype, superlatives, or fear |
| Responsible | Human judgment; measurable claims | Guarantees we cannot keep |
| Timeless | Language that still reads well in ten years | Trend-chasing jargon |

**Who we write for:** executives, technology leaders, quality professionals, and institutional decision makers.

Every page should answer:

1. Who is AOVIAS?
2. Why does AOVIAS exist?
3. Why should enterprise organizations trust AOVIAS?

## 2. Preferred terminology

| Prefer | Avoid / use carefully |
| --- | --- |
| institution, institutional | “customer” when the subject is a hospital, agency, or school (customer is fine in commerce UI) |
| platform | “suite,” “stack,” “ecosystem” as decoration |
| operator, operations team | “user” when the role is operational accountability |
| stewardship | “ownership” as a buzzword; ownership is fine for clear accountability |
| accountable judgment / human oversight | “AI-powered,” “intelligent automation” without purpose |
| engineering discipline | “world-class engineering,” “best-in-class” |
| durable, dependable, steady | “blazing fast,” “disruptive,” “game-changing” |
| AOVIAS Command / AOVIAS Studio (full name on first use in a section) | “Command™,” casual nicknames |
| Request a demo / Talk with AOVIAS | “Book a call,” “Get started free,” “Unlock” |

**Product identity**

- **Command** — operational control and institutional operations workspace  
- **Studio** — delivery workspace to design, refine, and ship institutional work  
- Do not swap their value props. Each product has a distinct job.

---

## 3. Words and phrases to avoid

### Startup / hype

disrupt, revolutionize, transform everything, unicorn, 10x, move fast and break things, hockey stick, growth hacking, crush it

### Empty AI / tech buzzwords

AI-powered (alone), next-gen, cutting-edge, seamless, frictionless, magic, smart (alone), leverage (as verb filler), synergetic, holistic synergy

### Fear selling

“Don’t get left behind,” “competitors are already…,” “survive the AI wave,” urgency without substance

### Unverified superlatives

best, #1, leading, unmatched, unprecedented — unless backed by a cited, approved fact

### Meta / developer voice on public pages

content model, template, placeholder, forthcoming, ATS integration, illustrative only (prefer honest: “Architecture overview” without apologizing)

## 4. Writing rules

### Headings

- Prefer outcome or purpose over cleverness  
- Title case for product names; sentence case for section headings unless the design system specifies otherwise  
- Avoid question headings that invent anxiety

### Body copy

- Lead with the institutional need, then the product response  
- Prefer active voice  
- Cut adjectives that do not change meaning  
- One claim per paragraph when making a trust statement

### CTAs

| Intent | Label pattern |
| --- | --- |
| Demo | Request a demo |
| Sales / enterprise | Talk with AOVIAS / Contact enterprise |
| Explore | Explore products / View Command / View Studio |
| Docs | Read documentation |
| Careers | View open roles |

Do not use “Learn more” when a specific destination exists.

### Product descriptions

Must cover, without repeating the same paragraph across products:

1. Problem  
2. Purpose  
3. Capabilities  
4. Who it is for  
5. Business value  
6. Operational value  
7. Platform relationship  
8. Roadmap posture (honest; no fake dates)

### Industry descriptions

Sector-specific challenges and institutional context — not generic “digital transformation” twins.

### Technical content

- Accurate; prefer “supports,” “designed for,” “intended to” over absolute guarantees  
- Separate evaluation guidance from production commitments

### Legal content

- Clear headings; plain language where appropriate  
- Single transparency notice: public draft for counsel review — **not legal advice** and **not counsel-approved**  
- Never invent regulatory certifications

### Careers

Attract people who value long-term engineering excellence. No culture-theater clichés.

## 5. Preferred vs avoid

| Avoid | Prefer |
| --- | --- |
| We leverage AI to revolutionize enterprise workflows. | We apply automation and AI where they improve accountable human judgment — with clear purpose and oversight. |
| The leading next-gen platform for digital transformation. | A focused platform that strengthens institutions through dependable software. |
| Seamless, frictionless experiences your users will love. | Interfaces designed for clarity so operators can act with confidence. |
| Book a demo and unlock growth today. | Request a demo to evaluate fit for your institution. |
| Coming soon — stay tuned! | Planned capability. This page describes direction; it does not commit to a schedule. |
| Best-in-class security. | Security and stewardship treated as fundamentals — measured claims, clear ownership. |

---

## 6. Claim rules (non-negotiable)

Do **not** invent:

- Named customers or logos  
- Certification marks or audit results without counsel/approval  
- Street addresses or personal leadership bios without verified facts  
- Product release dates or SLAs without product authority  
- Metrics presented as measured outcomes when they are qualitative intent

When facts are unavailable:

- Write philosophy and capability honestly  
- Use empty states without “coming soon” theater when a roster or map is not ready  
- List gaps in Outstanding Items for human review

---

## 7. Editorial review checklist

Before publishing any page:

1. Does this sound like AOVIAS?  
2. Would an enterprise executive trust this?  
3. Is every sentence necessary?  
4. Can anything be simplified?  
5. Does the content reinforce purpose rather than promotion?  
6. Are product names and roles consistent with this guide?  
7. Would this still read well in ten years?

If any answer is no — rewrite.
